What's new

Everything that changed, newest first. The newest build is 1.0.5 (build 87). Written as each change was made, so nothing here is a guess, and only in English for now.

13 August 2026

A phone calendar can be shared whole now. On the Us screen, under Phone calendars, each calendar shown there carries a second switch: share it with the other person. Turn it on and its events appear on the other phone and keep themselves current, thirty days back and four hundred ahead. A copy opened on the phone that shares it offers one button to take that one event off, and the choice is remembered. Nothing is ever written to the phone's own calendar; Clovie still only reads it.

Before that switch existed, single events got the same door: open an event that lives on your phone's own calendar and one button puts it on the calendar you share, where it syncs like anything typed in. If the copy already exists the button turns itself off, so a second tap cannot double the plan.

At the biggest text sizes the cycle screen stopped cutting things off. A fertile window that crosses into the next month wraps instead of running off the card, and the label and value share the room instead of fighting for one line.

The could-not-save message now appears in Portuguese on phones set to Portuguese. It was the one line that always came up in English.

12 August 2026

The striped testing block on the Us screen is gone. Add the example and Remove the example were tools for trying the app out, not settings, and they now exist only in development builds.

The "You usually buy" suggestions no longer learn from the example. Ticking off one of the example's groceries used to count as a real purchase, so the example's shopping kept being offered back long after the example itself was removed. Only what you tick off yourselves teaches the list now.

Back behaves the way Android promises. Pressing back on any tab returns to Home first and only leaves the app from Home, and the edge swipe now draws the screen behind while your finger is still on it, instead of committing blind.

The everyday moments answer the finger. Ticking off a shopping item, saving a set, finishing a swipe-delete and a new record each give the small standard buzz, and the phone's own touch-feedback setting is respected throughout.

An event added in Google Calendar shows up when you switch back to Clovie, instead of waiting for the app to be killed and reopened.

Turning the notification permission off no longer quietly erases every scheduled reminder. They stay put, silent while the permission is off, and sound again once it is back.

The food log left open across midnight wakes up on the new day instead of staying pinned to yesterday.

A pairing code on display is now a code that works. A code that went stale, or one whose invitation never finished being written, mends itself the moment the account screen shows it.

8 August 2026

A period stops saying it is happening once its days are over. An entry nobody closed used to count as under way for five days past the average length, so one logged on 30 July still said "Happening now" on 8 August, day ten of a five day period, and it said it under the date the next one is due. It now covers exactly the days the app already shades everywhere else, and while one is under way that line is about that one.

A finger held on the cycle ring reads it out day by day. The circle keeps its shape; the middle swaps today for whichever day is under your finger, and letting go puts it back.

The shopping list shows as many things as it used to. The button that moves something to another aisle had quietly made every row a third taller, so four of the fourteen things on the list fell off the bottom of the screen.

Recipes can be filtered by how long they take, under twenty minutes or under forty-five, worked out from the prep and cook times already on them. A recipe nobody timed stays out of those two rather than quietly passing. The hand-put Quick label is no longer offered when writing a recipe, because the minutes say it better, and one already on a recipe stays where it is and still shows.

Either of you can keep a cycle, or both of you, or neither. If your partner keeps one and you do not, there is still a way in from the Us screen and it says whose it is before it opens. Theirs is read-only from your side, and nothing of theirs is ever put on your home screen.

Your calendar and your reminders now only ever use your own period. Both people's are on both phones, and the calendar had been mixing the two into one prediction and colouring days that belonged to neither, while the reminders could nudge you about your partner's.

The plus and minus for servings is one control now, drawn and behaving the same way wherever it appears. The exercise search says the same thing in the same voice as every other empty list in the app.

Your best is marked with a star, in your own colour: on the best set of an exercise, on the set that just beat it, on the day it came from, and on the session in the list where it happened. Before this the app knew your record and only ever said so once, in the confetti at the end.

A set is written down the moment you save it. Backing out of a workout, a flat battery, or Android closing the app behind you now leave every set that was already in. Before this the whole session sat in memory until Done was pressed, and somebody lost a session that way, which is what started all of this.

Starting a workout opens a full page instead of a sheet that covered half the screen, and correcting an old session or starting from a routine open that same page.

Adding an exercise starts with the muscle groups, with a search box above them that looks across all of them at once and Recent underneath. Picking one opens that exercise's own page, with the weight and reps boxes and a SAVE that adds one set at a time, so the next set is an adjustment rather than a re-type, and with HISTORY and GRAPH beside it.

Any exercise can be renamed, from a pencil you can see rather than a long press nobody finds. Renaming one from the built-in list keeps every past workout pointing at it, so its history and its graph carry on. The new name shows on both phones, and the app says so before you save it.

Weights can be read in pounds. It is a setting on your own phone, and what is stored never changes, so one of you can read pounds while the other reads kilograms.

The theme, the accent, the hand-picked colours, the language and the record confetti are yours alone now. They used to travel between the two phones as one bundle, so changing the language on one changed it on the other. Updating keeps each phone looking exactly as it does today. Only the names, nicknames, colours and pronouns you set for each other are still shared.

Building a routine, a recipe or an event is a full page too, and backing out of one with something half-written asks before it throws it away.

7 August 2026

A whole-day event's reminder no longer pushes the word "Reminder" off its own row in the event editor. The choices there now say "Morning before" and "Evening before" instead of spelling the hour out, which is what made the row too wide to hold both.

A new event now comes with a reminder already on it, half an hour before if it has an hour, and nine in the morning the day before if it is a whole day. Before this a shared calendar stayed silent unless somebody remembered to set a reminder by hand on every single event. Any event can still be given a different one, or none at all.

The Us screen has a Notifications section: one switch for event reminders, the two times a new event is given, one switch for the period reminder, and the button that asks Android for permission if it has not been given yet. Every switch there belongs to that phone, so turning something off on one phone leaves the other one alone.

Whoever tracks a cycle now gets a quiet word at eight the evening before the app expects a period to start. It waits until three cycles have been logged, because before that the average moves around too much to be worth saying. It is worked out on the phone itself, so nothing new leaves it.

The licences list now includes Manrope, the font the app is set in, with its licence text.

Nothing to see: the public registration record for clovie.eu no longer carries a personal e-mail address.

Nothing to see: the privacy check that compares the app, the docs and the website can now stop a release instead of only warning about it.

6 August 2026

The home screen could greet you with a days-together count below zero, when the start date sat in the future because of a typo in setup or a phone whose clock had run ahead. It reads as no days together yet instead.

Joining a household could occasionally place both partners on the same side, mixing up whose workouts, cycle days, meals and weigh-ins belonged to whom. This is now caught and corrected the moment it happens, moving everything back to the right person automatically.

Each partner's own profile, name, nickname, avatar and pronoun, and the couple's shared preferences, theme, colours, language, now sync properly between phones instead of one phone's change being able to overwrite the other's.

The weight, exercise and gym progress charts can now be tapped or dragged along to see the exact value and date behind any point, instead of only showing a plain trend line.

An invite code for joining a household now expires after a while instead of working forever, so an old code shared by accident cannot still be used much later.

Fixed a bug where two phones in different time zones could disagree about which of two changes happened more recently, occasionally letting an older change overwrite a newer one.

1 August 2026

The app now correctly shows itself as version 1.0.1.

31 July 2026

The Us screen's nickname field is now labeled with a plain "Nickname" instead of a pronoun-specific sentence.

Cycle tracking's on/off toggle is now a full switch row instead of a small unlabeled chip, and onboarding now asks once, up front, whether you track a cycle, still changeable later from your own edit page.

The shopping list now offers live suggestions while you type, ranked by your own past purchases first.

Recipes, routines and workouts can now be reordered by dragging their ingredient, step, or exercise rows, the same way the shopping list already worked.

The gym screen is now five pages instead of three, Progress, Overview, Routines, History and Records, with the trend view split onto its own tab and the page-position dots back.

The phone-calendars count now says how many are "shown" instead of "synced", so the wording no longer sounds like a claim about data leaving the phone.

Whether you track a cycle now toggles from your own edit page, next to your pronoun, instead of sitting on the shared screen.

Add and remove example data now works on a real phone again, not just in development.

The example data now covers a full three months of history.

30 July 2026

A later month's early days no longer show the wrong cycle colour.

You can now rename a routine from a long press, the same way exercises already could.

Log Food now has a scan button. Point it at a barcode and it fills in the product automatically, or type the digits by hand if the camera can't read it or isn't allowed.

Logging food or adding a recipe ingredient now searches Open Food Facts as you type, on top of your own saved foods.

Colours now line up consistently: the gym time-window chips and the combined weekly workout total on the Today tab use a neutral colour instead of one person's, cycle mood chips match the symptom chips above them, message bubbles from both people are now coloured by who sent them, and the Us tab's milestones row has its own icon.

The Kitchen tab's filter chips (All, Favourites, and the category tags) now look like the recipe tag pills instead of a separate chip style.

On a recipe's page, "Start cooking" and "Put it on a day" now sit together right below the servings control, instead of at the very bottom past the ingredients and method.

On the Training tab, the muscle-bar counts (Legs, Shoulders, Arms) are no longer hidden behind the "+" button, and the 30 days/3 months/12 months/All time chips now sit in two even rows instead of an uneven 3-then-1 wrap.

The calendar's date title (for example "July 2026") now shows a small icon next to it, hinting that tapping it jumps back to today.

Row titles in Training and the shopping list are now a consistent size and weight, matching how event rows already looked.

Rows and cards that open something now show a small arrow, so it is clearer they can be tapped, in Training (weight, routines, workout history) and on Today (trip countdown, the shopping and gym cards).

How you switch whose events or training you're looking at, on the Calendar and Training tabs, has changed, and so has how you pick who an event belongs to when adding one.

Cook mode now uses the servings you scaled to, and the ingredients sheet scrolls.

New messages now show a mark until you've opened the thread.

Adding an item that is already on the shopping list no longer prints it twice. Naming a quantity the second time around updates the existing line.

Closing an exercise, muscle, equipment or unit picker while leaving the editor underneath it open no longer risks a crash.

You can now create a custom exercise directly from the exercise picker, complete with its own muscle group and equipment. Renaming it later from a long press keeps its logged history and personal records under the new name.

You can now drag a shopping item up or down within its aisle by its handle, and the new order sticks after a reload.

Long-pressing a shopping item now lets you move it to a different aisle from a sheet, and it stays there after a restart.

The shopping list now has a search box once you have more than six items on it. It matches a partial word across both the to-buy and bought sections, and clearing the search brings everything back.

Searching for an exercise, or logging one, now finds it whether or not you type the accent, in either language.

The calendar now shades an open period for roughly as many days as your own past periods have actually lasted, not a flat five, across the month, week and day views.

Opening the edit dialog on a period logged with a future start or end date no longer crashes, and picking either date still works normally for an ordinary entry.

At the largest system text size, the calendar's day numbers now grow legibly without clipping or spilling off screen, in month, week, day and year view and while switching between them.

If the calendar is left open overnight, or the app sits in the background across midnight, it now catches up to today on its own instead of getting stuck on yesterday.

If your phone loses signal mid-save, the app now keeps trying quietly in the background instead of waiting for you to touch something.

Fixed a security gap where one partner could have kicked the other out of the shared household. Nothing changes for normal use.

29 July 2026

The hidden game's ball now drops toward the paddle first, and every bounce off a wall, the ceiling, a brick, or the paddle makes a sound.

The secret heart-tap game's ball now eases in noticeably slower and launches gentler, on every life, not just the first.

Your name and pronoun now change from a small edit icon next to your own name on the Us screen, not by typing in place. Nobody can switch which phone they are after the first setup anymore.

Each person can now choose how they're referred to in the app, he/him, she/her, or they/them, with a small tap-to-change label on the Us screen. Defaults to he/him and she/her as before, and stays that way unless you tap it.

Each side of the couple can now only edit their own name, not their partner's. Nicknames stay editable by either phone, as before.

Swiping left or right in the gym section now works from anywhere on the page, including the empty space below a short list, not just directly over the visible content.

The hidden heart-tap game's ball now eases in slowly and settles in the middle of the screen instead of rushing to a spot by the paddle, and every life now starts at a gentler pace before building back up to full speed.

Fixed the hidden heart-tap game lagging badly when the ball gets on top of the bricks and bounces there breaking them from above. That still happens on purpose, it just does not slow the app down anymore.

Fixed the hidden heart-tap game's ball getting stuck bouncing rapidly between the paddle and the bricks. It now settles gently in the gap between them, both when it first drops in and after you lose a life.

The phone calendars section on the Us screen is tidier now. Instead of a switch for every calendar on the page, one row shows how many are synced and opens a short list to change them.

The Cycle section on the Us screen now has a switch. Turn it off and both the cycle card and the quick way to log a period disappear from your Today screen.

A slower, more deliberate swipe in the gym section now changes the page too. Before, only a quick flick worked.

Swiping in the gym section now moves between your own overview, routines, and history, instead of switching to your partner's stats. Seeing your partner's training is now a small link instead of a swipe.

The small pill that lets you say which side of the couple is you, on the names step of onboarding, is now easier to notice.

The delete-account warning no longer claims your partner's shared data disappears too, since it never did.

Cycle tracking is now personal to whoever is logging it, so your partner's phone no longer shows your entries as their own. A small link at the top of the cycle screen lets you check on your partner's cycle too, without being able to change it, and switch back to yours in one tap.

The hidden heart-tap game now appears right away instead of fading in slowly, so the ball drop and heart pulse are visible from the start.

The hidden heart-tap game's ball now picks up a little speed the longer you keep it going in one life, up to a cap, and returns to its normal starting speed each time you lose a life.

The home screen now always shows a way into the cycle tracker, even before your first entry, and the "add something" hint can be tapped to add it directly.

28 July 2026

The hidden heart-tap game now turns its whole welcome message into bricks, not just the title, and moves a bit faster.

There are three new accent colours to choose from, Slate, Navy and Teal. Onboarding also now asks once for your own name instead of asking for both, with a small dot in the corner if you need to say which side is you.

In Gym, you can now swipe left or right on the page to switch between your stats and your partner's.

You can now delete your account and everything tied to it for good, from Shared account under Us. There's also a new Sweet theme option, and Erase everything now reads as a plain button instead of a red warning.

Delete account now only shows up once you've paired with someone, not the moment you sign in.

The hidden heart game starts faster now, and the heart visibly turns into the ball as you tap into it instead of just appearing. The small side picker on the names page is now a labelled pill instead of a tiny hidden dot.

The date on Us is now a visible button instead of a plain line of text. The calendars card also now says you can switch off any you don't want synced, since that was already possible but wasn't obvious from the wording.

Sharing your invite now sends a real link. Tapping it on your partner's phone opens Clovie straight to Shared account with the code already filled in, instead of them having to type it.
