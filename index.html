Why Clovie exists

Clovie is built for two people who share a life, not for a household of ten on a plan with a monthly fee. One calendar, one shopping list, one recipe book, and a training log each. It started with the question two people ask each other most, what have we got on this week, and the answer belongs somewhere both of you can see.

One calendar, both of your plans

Every event is colour-coded, so whose it is is obvious at a glance. Anything already on your phone's own calendar shows right alongside it. Clovie only ever reads that, never touches it.

One list, added to from two phones

Add something from the sofa. It's already there when the other one's standing in the shop. Items sort into aisles as you type, so the list reads in the order you'll walk the store.

A recipe book that remembers what you cook

Save the ones you keep coming back to. Tag them by meal or mood, filter by how long they take, and turn any of them into a shopping list with one tap.

A training log, not a spreadsheet

Pick a movement, type the weight and the reps, save. Each set is written down as you save it, so a flat battery halfway through a session costs you nothing. Every movement keeps its own history and its own line on a graph, and your bodyweight sits alongside them.

Cycle tracking, right alongside everything else

Turned on for whichever of you wants it, from setup or from settings later. Turn it on and your partner can see it from their own side of the app, whether or not they keep a cycle of their own. Theirs is read-only; only the person it belongs to writes to it.

Local by default

Sign in together, and only the two of you can reach what you write. Skip that, and nothing leaves the phone at all, no account needed. Either way, nothing is sold and nothing watches how you use it.

Delete your account any time. Whoever you're paired with keeps their own copy exactly as it was. The full detail, including what the app asks the phone for, is on the privacy page.

Get Clovie

Android only, for now. It downloads straight from here rather than from a store, so your phone will check with you twice before it opens the file.

Get it for Android

You'll get version 1.0.5 (build 65). What changed.

  1. Tap the button. Your browser will warn you the file could be harmful. Choose Download anyway.
  2. Open it from your notifications. Android will say this source isn't allowed to install apps. Tap Settings, then turn the switch on for the browser you downloaded with.
  3. Go back and tap Install. If Play Protect offers to scan it first, let it.

Then open Clovie and invite the other person in, or start on your own first. This version won't update itself. Clovie is going onto the Play Store, and the one from there will.
